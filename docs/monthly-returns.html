Every cell is the index's % change from the previous month-end close to that month-end close (price index — dividends not included). The last column is the year's return: CY = Dec close vs previous Dec close, FY = Mar close vs previous Mar close; for the running year it's the year-to-date return and the current month (dashed cell) updates live with every trading day until the month closes. * marks a year where the index didn't exist for the full period (return measured from its first available month). vs Nifty 50 subtracts Nifty 50's same-month return from every cell, turning the heatmap into relative-strength (alpha). In Trailing returns every cell measures back from the latest close: 1D from the previous close, 1W from the rolling daily window, and 1M/3M/6M/1Y/2Y/3Y/5Y/10Y from the month-end close that many months back (so 1M is the same thing as the running month's MTD); columns marked * are annualised (CAGR), and LTP vs 52W is the distance below the index's 52-week high. NSE's per-index daily archive is bot-walled, so the daily window used by the 1W column builds up one trading evening at a time and that column stays blank until it reaches back a week. In the seasonality view the footer shows, for each calendar month, the average return across all years and the hit rate — the share of years that month closed positive. History: NSE's official index archive back to each index's base date (Nifty 50 & Nifty 500 from 1995, Nifty IT from 1996, most sectoral indexes from the early 2000s; back-computed by NSE before an index's launch date). Current month topped up on trading evenings from NSE's live index feed. Educational only — not investment advice.
